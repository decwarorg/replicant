# Playing Defense and Staying Alive

_At the end of this guide is a summary of the commands discussed._ 

Let's step through how to play defense and how to stay alive. This is a practical approach to the game and nice for beginners. It avoids too much stress when you're just starting to learn, because things can happen very fast and easily become overwhelming. Playing defense and staying alive gives you a solid set of responses to stressful situations. They give you a clear path and guideposts for what to do when the screen starts filling with damage reports and notifications of bad things happening. You want an automatic response, which for a beginner will often be to run away. Part of staying alive is the skill of retreating, but there's definitely more to it! If you happen to be in a game with very few players, there might be little action overall. You might not be attacked by other players, but you could still be targeted by the Romulan. The Romulan can be difficult to fight and poses a real challenge, especially as a beginner. If you get attacked by the Romulan, the best initial strategy is usually to run away. What we're going to discuss here makes things a bit more interesting, because the message isn't just to retreat every time.

Playing defense is actually a very useful strategy for your whole team. It's about protecting your side's bases. To keep it simple, let's start with protecting one base. The first step on this path is to pick a base, move to its area, and defend it. There are several advantages to this. When you're near the base you're defending, you can easily dock with it, and you should do that often. Docking with the base restores your strength. This in itself will keep you alive, simply staying near the base you are defending and being able to dock quickly. You can take a lot of damage and still survive simply because docking is so convenient. In large games with many players, some players need to focus on defending bases. When your side loses all of its bases, you are perilously close to losing the game. Therefore, having dedicated defenders to protect bases is a core part of an effective strategy.

So how does it work? Use the bases command to view your team's bases. Pick one of those bases, likely the nearest one, though not necessarily. You might prefer a base in a strong location, such as one of the corners of the galaxy rather than the center. If you try to defend a base in the center of the Galaxy, it's going to be more difficult. Look at the list of your bases, select one, and move towards it. Use the bases command followed by the move command. For now, that's all that's necessary. When using the move command, travel at a safe speed. For example, move four sectors on each turn. This avoids straining your warp engines. You can leave your shields on since you're heading straight to a base where you can dock to completely restore your health upon arrival. Move at a moderate speed directly toward the base you intend to protect. Once there, dock quickly to fully recover health, and you'll be fully prepared to defend.

The next step on this path comes once you've arrived at your primary base, docked, and restored your health. If nothing is currently happening and you have some time, pick a secondary base that is nearby. This gives you a reliable place to escape to. If you're the target of an all-out attack, your primary base can get wiped out and your ship will be damaged. In high-stress moments, you want a fallback position. Pick a secondary base and patrol between your primary and secondary bases. You are now on defensive patrol. You are defending your primary base, which serves as your main stand-and-fight location, while keeping the secondary base ready as a retreat option. In a disaster, you have somewhere to run and dock quickly. You're also actively helping your team by protecting two bases. Defending two bases makes great tactical sense. As you become a better player, you will eventually be able to defend three or more bases, but two is the ideal starting point. Center your activities around your primary base and maintain awareness of your secondary base. How to get there? What’s its status?

If you're at one base and the other comes under attack, you need to rush over immediately to defend it. Your patrol can be interrupted at any point by an attack, and your priority is to respond quickly. Think of this area as your personal zone, stretching between the two bases you are defending. By taking responsibility for what happens in your zone, you play an active, collaborative role for your team while learning about the game. You will learn how to use your sensors, run scans, process incoming information, and use your radio to communicate with teammates. Keep them updated on your actions, letting them know, for example, that you are patrolling and defending the two bases in the upper-left corner of the galaxy.

Now let's examine what staying alive really means and why having a base to dock with is so vital. It all comes down to energy. Your ship has a specific amount of energy, which acts like a battery that drains over time. You also have a separate secondary energy pool dedicated specifically to your shields. It is possible to transfer energy between these two reserves, ship energy and shield energy, and both are critical to your overall health. You burn energy whenever you take actions and will eventually run out of energy simply from moving around and firing phasers. Taking damage from enemy attacks also depletes your energy reserves. When involved in combat, you will likely have less energy remaining than you expect because energy also functions similarly to life points. When you take damage, you lose energy. There is a third number representing various types of damage. When hit, you lose energy points and accumulate damage points. The three core health numbers are primary ship energy, shield energy, and damage points. As your health degrades through energy loss and damage accumulation, docking with a base is by far the most effective way to recover. Use the status command regularly to check your ship's overall condition across all three numbers. When you dock, ship energy and shield energy recover while damage points are repaired. Docking also replenishes your supply of photon torpedoes.

In the big picture, the least stressful and most instructive way to learn combat is to stay near the bases you are defending. If attacked, dock regularly so you don't have to worry about your health dropping too low. In combat, docking quickly gives you a massive advantage over attackers. If you play smart and utilize base docking efficiently, it will take multiple enemy ships working together to take you down or destroy the base. In intense games, enemy players will coordinate team attacks. If they overwhelm your current base, escape and head along your retreat path to your secondary base within your zone. Move across your zone to your secondary base. While enemy ships may follow you, your teammates will notice you are under attack and will rush in to support your defense. By falling back, you buy valuable time for your teammates to reach your zone. This can spark a major fleet battle as multiple teammates engage the incoming enemy forces. Large battles often unfold around bases in this pattern. A raiding party attacks, forces you back from the first base to the secondary, and gives your team enough time to reinforce the defense.

Here are a few tips for defending against incoming enemy ships. Stay aware of nearby stars around your base using the scan command. Think of nearby stars as landmines surrounding your base that you can detonate with well-placed torpedoes. When engaging an enemy ship directly, pepper them with phaser fire first to weaken their shields. Once you've hit them with two or three phaser blasts to lower their shields, follow up with photon torpedoes. Mixing phaser fire and photon torpedoes is an effective combination. Avoid over-relying on just one weapon type, use both together.

## Summary of the commands discussed

DECWAR accepts the shortest unambiguous command abbreviation, usually two letters. For commands that need a location, coordinates are vertical position followed by horizontal position. You can give an absolute location, such as `M 37 45`, or use `R` for a relative move, such as `M R 4 -5` (four sectors up and five left from your current position). Many players set relative coordinates as default via the command `SET ICDEF REL` so the `R` is not needed and `M 4 -5` or `PH 4 -5` is enough. This is often done in the startup DECWAR.INI file.

- `BA` lists your friendly bases and their locations. Use `BA CL` to see the closest friendly base. Choose a base to defend, then move toward its listed coordinates.
- `M <vpos> <hpos>` moves using warp engines. Warp 4 is the maximum safe speed; the distance moved is the larger of the vertical and horizontal coordinate changes. For example, `M R 4 0` moves four sectors up. Warp 5 and 6 can damage the engines. Shields double movement energy use, so keep an eye on your supplies.
- `DO` docks at an adjacent friendly base or planet, refueling, rearming, and repairing the ship. `DO ST` docks and then reports your status. Dock regularly while defending; a base restores more resources per turn than a planet.
- `ST` shows your full status, including location, ship and shield energy, damage, torpedoes, and radio condition. Use `ST E D SH` to check energy, damage, and shields, or `ST L` to check your location.
- `SC` scans the area up to ten sectors around you. Use `SC 4` for a smaller scan, or `SC W` to mark sectors within range of enemy bases and planets as dangerous. Stars appear as `*`; ships, bases, and planets use the symbols listed by `HELP SCAN`.
- `PH <vpos> <hpos>` fires phasers at one coordinate within ten sectors. For example, `PH 37 45` targets sector 37-45. A normal phaser blast uses 200 ship energy; an optional energy amount can be supplied, from 50 to 500.
- `TO 1 <vpos> <hpos>` fires one photon torpedo toward a coordinate; for example, `TO 1 37 45`. Torpedoes travel along a path, may be deflected, and can hit something other than the intended target. They can also make a nearby star go nova, so take care when firing near your base or teammates. Phaser blasts can weaken an enemy's shields before you commit torpedoes.
- `TE ALL;<message>` sends a message to every ship by radio. Replace `ALL` with a ship name or a team such as `FRIENDLY` to narrow the audience. `RA ON` enables your radio if it has been turned off; `ST` reports whether it is on.

For command-specific help during a game, type `HELP` followed by the command, such as `HELP MOVE` or `HELP TORPEDOES`.
